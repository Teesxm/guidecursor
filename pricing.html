FUNDING & PILOT MODEL

Workplace accessibility,not freemium SaaS.

The current business model is an annual licence per user, paid by the employer or potentially through a UWV work provision. Specialist assistive-technology suppliers and trainers are the intended adoption channel. The paper does not set a commercial price yet, so this site no longer invents one.

NO LIVE CHECKOUT · NO CONSUMER PLAN TIERS
01 · LICENCE MODEL

One user.
One annual workplace licence.

GuideCursor is positioned as a work provision for visually impaired macOS users. Pricing should be validated through a pilot rather than guessed before we know support, training and compatibility costs.

PLANNED COMMERCIAL MODEL

Annual licence per user

Paid by an employer or through an eligible work-provision route. The licence would cover the GuideCursor software, supported workflows, updates and the support needed to keep the tool usable as macOS and applications change.

Buyer / funderEmployer or UWV
End userWorking-age person with visual impairment using macOS
DistributionAssistive-tech suppliers & trainers
PriceTo validate during pilot
WHY THIS ROUTE

Fits existing support structures

UWV already provides work provisions for people with a visual impairment, while employers and specialist suppliers already buy, install and teach assistive software.

Funding fitWorkplace provision
Training fitExisting specialist channels
User fitIntroduced in a familiar support setting
02 · ROUTE TO ADOPTION

From pilot partner
to workplace deployment.

The first step is not a self-service purchase funnel. It is evidence from intended users and a partner organisation that can support a workplace pilot.

1 · RECRUIT

Work with specialist organisations

Visio, Bartiméus and Oogvereniging are potential recruitment partners for low-vision and blind test users.

2 · PILOT

Partner organisation + innovation funding

The course MVP can become a follow-up pilot only with external support, intended-user testing and a partner willing to trial it.

3 · INTRODUCE

Employers, UWV, suppliers and trainers

Successful pilots can feed into workplace funding and specialist installation/training channels instead of a generic consumer app store funnel.

03 · ONGOING COSTS

Support grows with compatibility,
not only usage.

Local processing keeps per-request costs low. The expensive part of a serious assistive product is maintaining reliable behaviour as supported applications and macOS change.

macOS changesRetest permissions, accessibility APIs and guidance behaviour after platform updates.
Application compatibilitySupport cost grows as more workflows and applications are added.
Model updatesRe-test target accuracy and response time whenever the model or candidate pipeline changes.
Support & trainingAccessible documentation, user support and specialist training are part of the product cost.
04 · WHAT THE PILOT MUST LEARN

Price follows evidence.

Before setting a credible annual fee, the team needs evidence about user benefit, training needs, local-model hardware requirements and the maintenance burden per supported application.

No fake €8 / €16 tiers anymore.

The previous concept site used consumer-style Free, Personal, Pro and Organization plans. Those no longer match the paper’s buyer, funding route or scope and have been removed from the current product story.

USER VALUEDoes GuideCursor improve task success, errors or time without reducing control?
DELIVERY COSTHow much training and support does one deployment require?
TECHNICAL COSTWhich Macs can run the preferred local model acceptably?
COMPATIBILITY COSTHow much work is needed to support each additional application?

First prove the workplace value.

GuideCursor’s commercial model should be grounded in the pilot, not a polished checkout page that implies a product is already for sale.
